# Power-up art

Each power-up and curse has one illustration, by slug:
`public/images/power-ups/<slug>.svg`. A slug shared across seasons shares
its picture. The SVGs are generated by `scripts/power-up-art/draw.py`, so
change the script, never the SVG files.

## The target style

Isometric technical drawings, in the site's VERKSTED direction: a
Teenage Engineering manual or an engineer's exploded view, not an icon set.
They should look drafted: considered, quiet, with one thing that catches the eye.

- **Canvas:** 243x162, the surface colour with a faint 20px grid (the
  generator draws both). The art sits in a ~160x100 area centred near
  (121, 81). No baselines, no text.
- **Projection:** every object is isometric (30°). Nothing is drawn flat,
  including swords, paper and barrier arms.
- **Line:** `--color-text-muted` for power-ups, `--color-accent` for curses.
  Two weights only: about 1.8 for outlines, 0.55–0.8 for detail and drafting
  marks (dimension lines, dashed leaders, motion dashes).
- **Solids:** the top face is `--color-bg`. The front-left face is hatched.
  The front-right face is `--color-display` for power-ups and
  `--color-border-accent` for curses. A face that carries a glyph (pips,
  tally marks, a 0, a ?) stays plain, so the glyph reads.
- **Highlight:** `--color-text` marks the punchline only, meaning the one
  element that says what the rule does (the bet, the fudged figure, the
  impact, the zeros). Use it once or twice per piece, never as decoration.
- **Palette:** site tokens only (the constants at the top of `draw.py`).

## The process

1. **Read the rule** in the season's `power-ups.ts`. Name its punchline in
   one sentence: what changes about points or placement. The picture has
   to say that sentence at ~240px wide, not just "gambling" or
   "two-sided".
2. **Pick concrete objects** from the shared vocabulary where you can:
   - place blocks or podium steps (descending heights mean placements)
   - coins and chips, dice and pawns
   - a barrier arm, a net, cards, a gavel
   - arrows for gain (up) and loss (down)
   - dimension lines for "twice" or "equal"

   Curses can mirror a power-up (`tax_scene(curse)` draws both tax
   evasions). If the slug already has art from another season, reuse it.

3. **Draw it:** add a function to `draw.py`, register it in `ART`, and point
   the slug's `imagePath` in `power-ups.ts` at `/images/power-ups/<slug>.svg`.
   Then run:

   ```
   python scripts/power-up-art/draw.py <slug>
   node scripts/power-up-art/preview.mjs
   ```

   The preview prints each piece's ink size and centre. It writes a contact
   sheet and 3x renders to `%TEMP%/power-up-art/`. Open both with Read:
   judge craft at 3x and readability on the sheet next to its siblings.

4. **Get an independent review.** Spawn a fresh agent as a demanding art
   director. Give it:
   - the sheet and the 3x PNG paths
   - this style section
   - what each piece must communicate

   Ask it for craft and "reads" scores out of 10, concrete fixes in
   artboard coordinates, set consistency, and a ship verdict. Tell it to
   judge only and not edit. Iterate with the same agent (SendMessage), so it
   checks its earlier notes were applied, until it says ship. Render each
   round to a fresh folder, so it never reads a file you're rewriting.

5. **Hand over for the taste check.** Show Jakob the sheet. The judge's
   approval is a floor, not sign-off.

## Building blocks in `draw.py`

Coordinates: `Iso(ox, oy, k)` maps (x, y, z) to the screen, with x running
down-right, y down-left and z up. `k` scales a piece. The front-left face
is the +y face and the front-right is the +x face. Draw back to front:
smaller x + y first.

- **`Art`:**
  - shapes: `poly`, `line`, `dashed`, `path`, `circle`
  - fills: `hatch()` returns a pattern; `shadow()` draws a hatched ground
    shadow
  - solids: `box()` draws a solid with the face rules applied; `faces()`
    returns the face polygons when you need custom fills
  - `ring()` gives a circle in the x, y or z plane
- **Objects:** `coin`, `die` with `PIPS`, `pawn`, `cylinder_x`, and `hull`
  for silhouettes built from rings.
- **Marks:** `arrow` and `arrow_head`, `bezier`, `trim` (shortens a path so
  it stops short of what it points at), `dim_v`.
- **Glyphs on faces:** `face_left` and `face_right` map 0–1 glyph
  coordinates onto a face. Use them with `glyph_q`, `glyph_zero` and
  `glyph_minus`.

## Faults reviews kept finding

Check these before asking for review:

- An arrow or path ending in empty space, or running into a vertex. It
  should land on its target, with a small gap (`trim`).
- A path running along a same-coloured edge, where it disappears.
- Tangents: two shapes touching by a hair. Either overlap them clearly or
  open a 4px gap.
- Objects floating with no ground or contact, unless the piece is about
  falling or flying.
- Glyphs on hatched faces; highlight spent on incidental bits.
- One flat (non-isometric) object in an isometric scene.
- Real-world errors: dice faces that are opposite each other (they sum to 7) can't both show.
- Pieces off-centre or much heavier than their siblings. Compare on the
  sheet, not alone.
